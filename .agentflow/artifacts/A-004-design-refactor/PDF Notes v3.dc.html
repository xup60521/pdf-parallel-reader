Rainfall interception in mixed hedgerows

Hedgerows on working farmland intercept a measurable share of rainfall before it reaches the field surface. Over three seasons we recorded throughfall beneath fourteen hedge sections of differing composition and age, alongside open-field controls at twenty metres.

Interception varied more with structural density than with species mix, though hawthorn-dominant sections held consistently more water in leaf than blackthorn or field maple at comparable widths.

This paper reports the throughfall measurements, the gauge layout, and a first estimate of the water budget attributable to hedge structure alone.

2. Gauge layout and method

Each section carried five tipping-bucket gauges on a transect perpendicular to the hedge line, at 0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 8 metres from the centre of the stem base. Gauges were levelled monthly and cross-calibrated against the station reference.

Figure 2 — transect gauge positions

Rainfall totals were binned by event rather than by day, with an event defined as precipitation separated by at least six dry hours. Events under 1 mm were discarded, leaving 214 usable events across the study period.

Canopy density was scored from hemispherical photographs taken at each gauge position in July of each year.

3. Results

Mean interception across all sections was 18.4% of open-field rainfall, ranging from 9.1% in the youngest blackthorn section to 27.6% in a mature hawthorn hedge of 3.2 m width.

Table 1 — interception by section

Distance from the stem base explained most of the within-section variance, with throughfall approaching open-field totals by four metres in all but two sections.
